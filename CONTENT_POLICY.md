# Daily brief content policy

## Purpose

Publish one useful, readable AI brief per day. The aim is to follow relevant developments and demonstrate a well-designed information workflow, not to manufacture activity through empty commits.

## Required structure

Every post must include:

- a clear headline;
- a short opening grounded in the selected source;
- `Why this matters`;
- `My perspective`;
- `What I want to explore next`;
- `A point to keep in mind`;
- a direct link to the original source; and
- an automation disclosure.

## Voice

- Natural, concise and professional.
- Written in clear British English.
- Thoughtful rather than promotional.
- First person is permitted only for verified interests, experience and future intentions.
- Avoid exaggerated language such as “revolutionary”, “game-changing” and “this changes everything” unless a source-specific reason is clearly explained.

## Truthfulness rules

An automated post must not say:

- “I tested this.”
- “I built this.”
- “I implemented this.”
- “I deployed this.”
- “Today I learned this.”

Those statements require a genuine manual update based on work that actually happened.

The system may say:

- “This connects with my interest in RAG and healthcare AI.”
- “I want to explore how this could be evaluated safely.”
- “My existing work makes this especially relevant to my learning direction.”

## Source rules

- Prefer the original organisation, project documentation or research paper.
- Always link directly to the source.
- Never copy a full article or long passage.
- Do not invent dates, benchmarks, model capabilities, study results or quotations.
- If the source is unclear or validation fails, publish nothing and let the recovery run try again.
